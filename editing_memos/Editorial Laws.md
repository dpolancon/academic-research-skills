
#  Consolidated editorial laws

## Law 1

Preserve the conceptual spine.

## Law 2

Do not preserve the visible over-management of that spine.

## Law 3

Keep the architecture, but reduce the reminders that architecture exists.

## Law 4

Let the sentence carry the pressure before adding commentary about what the sentence is doing.

## Law 5

Prefer contradiction, mechanism, and historical differentiation over procedural narration.

## Law 6

Protect the bounded argumentative ethic, but do not let caution suffocate momentum.

## Law 7

Do not neutralize adversarial motion when a paragraph advances through theoretical disagreement, historical correction, or explanatory displacement.

## Law 8

In historical sections, concepts must appear as part of a field, not as labels hovering above it.

## Law 9

Use chronology as a stabilizer whenever conceptual density begins to overload the prose.

## Law 10

Particularity is not a concession. It is one of the main vehicles of explanatory force in historical writing.
