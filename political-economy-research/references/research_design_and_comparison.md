## 1. Scope & Specialization Predicate

`M1` governs the construction of comparative-historical research designs and the adjudication of whether cases are analytically independent (`PARALLEL COMPARISON`), mutually constituted/linked (`RELATIONAL EXPLANATION`), or a combination of both (`HYBRID`).

### 1.1 Specialization Over Generic Comparative Templates
[`deep-research/references/methodology_patterns.md`](file:///c:/ReposGitHub/academic-research-skills/deep-research/references/methodology_patterns.md) (Pattern 2) provides a generic, atemporal, parallel-only comparative case study template for contemporary program or policy comparisons. `M1` specializes rather than duplicates that baseline whenever any of the following **Historical-Relational Specialization Predicates** hold:
1. **Temporal Scope as an Analytical Variable:** The inquiry spans distinct historical periods or institutional transitions (`M2`).
2. **Sequence-Sensitive Inference:** The causal argument depends on the temporal order, duration, or timing of institutional and political-economic processes (`M3`).
3. **Contested Case Independence (Galton's Problem):** Cases may be connected through transnational trade, finance, monetary hierarchy, geopolitical coercion, or shared systemic shocks.
4. **Historical/Documentary Evidentiary Base:** Supporting claims requires archival, institutional, or reconstructed statistical evidence governed by [`historical_source_criticism_protocol.md`](file:///c:/ReposGitHub/academic-research-skills/shared/protocols/historical_source_criticism_protocol.md).

## 2. Comparative-Historical Research Design (`O1`)

### 2.1 Mandatory Design Reasoning Obligations
Every comparative-historical inquiry must establish:
1. **Explanandum Specification:** Define the exact historical outcome, divergence, transformation, or persistence to be explained, stating its contrast space (*"Why did outcome $Y_1$ occur in case $A$ during period $T$, rather than outcome $Y_2$ observed in case $B$ or period $T-1$?"*).
2. **Unit of Analysis & Case Construction:** Specify what constitutes a case (nation-state, subnational region, industrial sector, firm network, monetary regime, labor negotiation episode) and ensure conceptual equivalence across cases and periods.
3. **Scope Conditions:** State the institutional, structural, and temporal boundary conditions under which the proposed explanation is claimed to hold. Generalization beyond stated scope conditions must be argued on structural grounds, never assumed.
4. **Case-Selection Rationale & Selection-Bias Guard:**
   - Justify case selection on purposive analytical grounds (e.g., diverse outcomes across similar structural antecedents, crucial cases for a specific mechanism, or core–periphery nodes within a shared system).
   - **No Uncontrolled Selection on the Dependent Variable:** Selecting only positive cases of an outcome ($Y = 1$) cannot establish cross-case causal sufficiency or necessity; if all cases share the outcome, leverage must come from **within-case longitudinal variation** across periods (`M2`) or **within-case process tracing** against rival mechanisms (`M3`).
5. **Within-Case vs. Cross-Case Inference Allocation:** Explicitly declare what cross-case comparison establishes (identifying structural patterns, bounding scope conditions, eliminating invariant explanations) versus what within-case process tracing establishes (`M3`: demonstrating that a specific causal mechanism actually operated in sequence).

## 3. Comparative-Relational Analysis (`O4`)

### 3.1 The Three Comparison Modes

```text
PARALLEL COMPARISON
    Cases are treated as analytically independent units. Variation in outcomes
    is explained by variation in domestic or case-internal conditions.
    Admissible ONLY when cross-case interdependence is absent, negligible for
    the explanandum, or explicitly controlled.

RELATIONAL EXPLANATION
    Cases are treated as connected or mutually constituted through structural,
    institutional, or transactional relations. Outcomes in one case are explained
    at least in part by the case's position within, or interaction through,
    verified linkages with other cases or a shared asymmetric system.

HYBRID COMPARISON
    Combines parallel comparison of domestic/case-internal institutional configurations
    with explicit modeling of common systemic shocks or bilateral/network linkages
    that transmit constraints or resources across cases.
```

### 3.2 Structural Evidence Predicates for Mode Selection (No Universal Numeric Thresholds)

> **CORE PRINCIPLE:** Mode selection between `PARALLEL`, `RELATIONAL`, and `HYBRID` is a **structural evidence-predicate problem, NEVER a universal numeric threshold** (such as an arbitrary trade-to-GDP percentage or capital-flow cutoff).

Evaluate three structural predicates before choosing a comparison mode:
1. **Constitutive or Causal Linkage Predicate:** Are the cases connected through concrete cross-border or inter-unit channels such that an actor's decision or an institution's trajectory in Case $B$ was directly shaped by actions, flows, or constraints originating in Case $A$?
2. **Common System Exposure Predicate (Galton's Problem):** Are the cases simultaneously exposed to a shared systemic shift (e.g., global interest-rate shock, commodity price collapse, international monetary regime breakdown) such that parallel trajectories reflect common external transmission rather than independent domestic causes?
3. **Asymmetric Hierarchy Predicate:** Do the cases occupy asymmetric positions within an institutional, monetary, financial, or geopolitical hierarchy where one unit's policy autonomy is structurally conditioned by another's rules or balance-sheet claims?

### 3.3 Required Substantive Evidence for `RELATIONAL EXPLANATION`
A `RELATIONAL` or `HYBRID` claim is an empirical historical claim like any other: **theoretical assertions of dependency, imperialism, or center–periphery hierarchy never substitute for historical evidence that the relation existed and operated.**

Substantiating a relational explanation requires protocol-assessed evidence (`historical_source_criticism_protocol.md`) across one or more of the following ten relational transmission channels:

| Relational Channel Code | Substantive Linkage Domain | Examples of Required Historical / Documentary / Flow Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| `TRADE` | **Trade Linkages** | Bilateral export/import dependence, tariff retaliation records, preferential trade agreements, terms-of-trade shock transmission. |
| `FINANCE` | **Cross-Border Finance & Debt** | Sovereign lending contracts, syndicate bank exposure, capital flight records, credit-rating or refinancing freezes, debt-service squeeze. |
| `OWNERSHIP` | **Foreign Ownership & Control** | Multinational subsidiary equity control, profit repatriation ledgers, board directives from parent headquarters, enclave concession contracts. |
| `POLICY_TRANSMISSION` | **Policy Transmission & Conditionality** | IMF/World Bank letters of intent, structural adjustment tranches, bilateral aid conditionality memoranda, cross-border regulatory harmonization. |
| `INSTITUTIONAL_DEPENDENCE` | **Institutional Dependence** | Extraterritorial legal jurisdiction, currency board rules tied to a foreign central bank, colonial/post-colonial administrative carryover. |
| `GEOPOLITICAL_HIERARCHY` | **Geopolitical Hierarchy & Coercion** | Diplomatic cables documenting sanctions threats, security pact conditions, military intervention, or strategic embargo enforcement. |
| `MIGRATION` | **Migration & Labor Regimes** | Cross-border labor recruitment treaties, remittance channels, skilled-labor drain, or deportations altering domestic labor markets. |
| `TECHNOLOGY_TRANSFER` | **Technology Transfer & IP Regimes** | Licensing restrictions, capital-goods import bottlenecks, patent enforcement treaties, or foreign technical mission records. |
| `MONETARY_RELATIONS` | **Monetary Relations & Currency Hierarchy** | Reserve-currency settlement constraints, central bank swap lines, sterling/dollar/franc zone reserve-pooling rules, interest-rate pass-through. |
| `COMMODITY_CHAIN` | **Commodity-Chain Dependence** | Upstream/downstream monopsony pricing, global value-chain subcontracting standards, logistics/shipping chokepoints. |

### 3.4 Guardrails Against Both Parallel and Relational Failure Modes
- **Against Naïve Parallelism (`IGNORED_INTERDEPENDENCE`):** If two cases exhibit similar trajectories or policy shifts, the analyst must test whether they are actually independent (`PARALLEL`) or responding to a shared shock / bilateral linkage (`RELATIONAL`/`HYBRID`). Treating linked cases as independent observations triggers the `IGNORED_INTERDEPENDENCE` downgrade in `M4`.
- **Against Unfalsifiable Relationalism:** If a prompt or theory presumes that external/center–periphery relations determined a domestic outcome, but no documentary or flow evidence demonstrates that the relational channel operated during the period and constrained domestic decision-makers, `M1` refuses to ratify `RELATIONAL` determination as an established finding and issues an **Evidence Request** (§5).

## 4. Supported vs. Mandatory Design Vocabularies & Artifact Serialization

In accordance with **`CAPABILITY SUPPORT ≠ UNIVERSAL APPLICATION REQUIREMENT`**:

- **Mandatory Reasoning Obligations:** Explanandum and contrast specification, case/unit definition, scope conditions, independence vs. relational linkage evaluation, and initial rival-explanation inventory.
- **Supported (Optional) Design Vocabularies:**
  - *Mill's Methods of Agreement and Difference* (supported as a baseline heuristic for `PARALLEL` designs, with explicit recognition of small-$N$ equifinality and Galton's problem);
  - *Skocpol & Somers Comparative Logics* (`parallel demonstration of theory`, `contrast of contexts`, `macro-causal analysis`);
  - *Tilly's Comparison Types* (`individualizing`, `universalizing`, `variation-finding`, `encompassing`);
  - *McMichael / Hart Relational & Incorporated Comparison* (supported as optional conceptual vocabulary for `RELATIONAL` mode).
- **Supported (Optional) Artifact Serialization (`A1` & `A5`):**
  - High-stakes research designs, dissertation chapters, and formal evaluations may serialize `A1` (**Comparative-Historical Design Brief**) and `A5` (**Case-Relation Map**, when `RELATIONAL` or `HYBRID` mode applies) using the schemas below. Short or exploratory consultations may present the same reasoning compactly in analytical prose.

### 4.1 Supported Artifact Schema: `A1 Comparative-Historical Design Brief` (`OPTIONAL SERIALIZATION`)
- `explanandum`: Outcome/variation to explain and explicit contrast space.
- `cases`: Selected cases and unit of analysis.
- `case_selection_rationale`: Analytical rationale and selection-bias checks.
- `temporal_scope`: Start/end bounds (linked to `M2` Periodization Ledger when analytical).
- `scope_conditions`: Structural/institutional boundary conditions of the claim.
- `comparison_mode`: `SINGLE_CASE` | `PARALLEL` | `RELATIONAL` | `HYBRID` + structural predicate justification.
- `inference_strategy`: Division of labor between cross-case comparison and within-case process tracing.
- `rival_explanations_inventory`: Competing hypotheses to be operationalized in `M3`.
- `evidence_plan`: Required documentary/quantitative evidence per rival.
- `composition_plan`: Required interfaces with `applied-econometrics-pe`, `heterodox-macro-modeling`, or `deep-research`.
- `project_parameters_used`: Any workspace overlay inputs, explicitly tagged as project parameters.

### 4.2 Supported Artifact Schema: `A5 Case-Relation Map` (`CONDITIONAL / OPTIONAL SERIALIZATION`)
*(Produced only when `comparison_mode` is `RELATIONAL` or `HYBRID`, or when case independence is contested.)*
- `case_pair_or_network`: Connected units ($A \to B$ or systemic node).
- `relation_channel`: Channel from §3.3 (`TRADE`, `FINANCE`, `OWNERSHIP`, `POLICY_TRANSMISSION`, `INSTITUTIONAL_DEPENDENCE`, `GEOPOLITICAL_HIERARCHY`, `MIGRATION`, `TECHNOLOGY_TRANSFER`, `MONETARY_RELATIONS`, `COMMODITY_CHAIN`).
- `direction_and_asymmetry`: Symmetric interdependence vs. hierarchical asymmetry.
- `period_of_operation`: Historical interval during which the linkage operated (`M2`).
- `evidence_for_relation`: Protocol-assessed documentary and flow evidence.
- `hypothesized_mechanism_link`: How the relation altered domestic actor incentives or institutional constraints (`M3`).
- `independence_implication`: Why naive `PARALLEL` comparison is invalid or requires `HYBRID` controls.

## 5. Structured Evidence Request Interface (`PE → deep-research`)

Because `political-economy-research` never searches or fabricates sources, any evidentiary gap discovered during design or relational mapping produces a structured **Evidence Request** routed to `deep-research`:
- `request_id`: Identifier linked to the design gap or `A4` matrix row.
- `discriminating_purpose`: Which competing hypotheses or relational predicates the requested evidence would adjudicate.
- `expected_trace`: Specific documentary, archival, or statistical records that should exist if the hypothesis holds.
- `likely_repositories_or_series`: Archival fonds, ministry records, legislative transcripts, or historical statistical compendia to search.
- `claim_type`: Target claim category (`OCCURRENCE`, `TIMING`, `DECISION_PROCESS`, `RELATION`, etc.) for protocol assessment.

## 6. Rotated Non-Canonical Illustrations (`ILLUSTRATION — NON-CANONICAL`)

1. **Illustration 1 (19th-Century European Agrarian & Tariff Coalitions — `HYBRID` Mode):**
   - *Design:* Comparing responses of British, German, and Danish agricultural and industrial coalitions to the 1870s–1880s transatlantic grain price shock.
   - *Mode Justification:* All three cases face a **common systemic shock** (`COMMODITY_CHAIN` / global grain transport revolution — Galton's problem), invalidating pure unconditioned isolation, while domestic institutional coalitions (landed estate structures vs. peasant cooperatives vs. free-trade industrial hegemony) explain the divergent policy responses (`HYBRID`).
2. **Illustration 2 (Post-1997 East Asian Financial & Corporate Restructuring — `RELATIONAL` vs. `PARALLEL` Contrast):**
   - *Design:* Comparing South Korea's IMF-conditioned capital-account liberalization with Malaysia's 1998 selective capital controls.
   - *Mode Justification:* Requires `HYBRID`/`RELATIONAL` modeling across `FINANCE` (short-term dollar bank debt roll-over freeze) and `POLICY_TRANSMISSION` (IMF standby negotiation records vs. non-IMF domestic liquidity management).
3. **Illustration 3 (1980s West African Monetary Union vs. Autonomous Currency Neighbors — `RELATIONAL` Mode):**
   - *Design:* Comparing fiscal adjustment in CFA Franc Zone members versus neighboring non-member states during the 1980s commodity slump.
   - *Mode Justification:* `INSTITUTIONAL_DEPENDENCE` and `MONETARY_RELATIONS` (operations account rules with the French Treasury and fixed convertibility guarantees) directly constituted the fiscal and monetary constraint space of member states.
