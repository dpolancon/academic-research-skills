## 1. HIERARCHICAL DIVISION OF EPISTEMIC AUTHORITY

To prevent circular reasoning, concept conflation, and unearned claims of priority, all propositions must be anchored in their appropriate layer of authority:

1. **Tier 1: External Scholarly Literature (`TIER_A` / `TIER_B` / `TIER_C`)**
   - *Authority:* The original published primary text.
   - *Scope:* Authoritative for historical debates, theoretical models, and empirical findings produced by outside scholars.
   - *Constraint:* Outside sources do not own the current manuscript's analytical reformulation.

2. **Tier 2: Foundational Methodological Predecessors (`TIER_D_INTERNAL_AUTHORITY`)**
   - *Authority:* The author's prior dissertation, foundational monograph, or working paper.
   - *Scope:* Authoritative internally for mathematical derivations, concept genealogy, and methodological lineage.
   - *Publication Ownership Rule:* Internal methodological lineage governs audit fidelity, but is not cited in the manuscript as an external predecessor when the current manuscript claims publication ownership of the contribution.

3. **Tier 3: Executable Data / Code Repositories (`TIER_D_INTERNAL_AUTHORITY`)**
   - *Authority:* Active project code and data pipelines.
   - *Scope:* Authoritative for variable definitions, data series boundaries, accounting transforms, and numerical calibrations.

4. **Tier 4: Manuscript Analytical Governance (`AUTH_MANUSCRIPT_GOVERNANCE`)**
   - *Authority:* The current paper's ratified conceptual architecture.
   - *Scope:* Authoritative for the paper's analytical spine, core research questions, and synthesis boundaries.
