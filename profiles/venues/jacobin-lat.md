# Venue Editorial Profile: Jacobin América Latina

This profile codifies the editorial standards, headline constraints, and rhetorical register of **Jacobin América Latina**, calibrated on the direct editorial directives of **Martín Arboleda**.

It serves strictly as a calibration input to [`public-scholarship`](../../public-scholarship/SKILL.md) for public political-economy essays, opinion columns, and historical interventions.

---

## 1. Editorial Authority & Core Analytical Framing

### 1.1 Analytical Framing: Bourgeois Economics vs. Critique of Political Economy
- **Analytical Terrain:** Do not reduce economic conflicts to the superficial parliamentary or electoral axis of *"la derecha vs. la izquierda"*. Situate debates on structural, class, and institutional terrain.
- **Not a Universal Substantive Thesis:** This is an analytical framing orientation that opposes technocratic naturalization; it is not a pre-determined substantive conclusion or empirical thesis forced onto every topic.
- **The Epistemological Conflict:** Contrast orthodox and structural explanations where relevant:
  - **La economía burguesa (ortodoxa, neoclásica, monetarista):** Naturalizes capitalist social relations, conceals the exploitation of labor, and treats inflation, austerity, or financial crises as technical market failures or moral punishments for popular spending.
  - **La crítica de la economía política (marxiana, poskeynesiana, estructuralista):** Demystifies capitalist relations by demonstrating that prices, money, debt, and crises are the material expressions of class struggle over the surplus, asymmetric market power, and international financial subordination.

### 1.2 The Role of History: Key to the Present
Historical episodes (such as the Unidad Popular in Chile, the Mexican Revolution, or Peronism) are not examined as antiquarian commemorations or bibliographic disputes between historians. They are analyzed because they unpack living structural contradictions of the present.

## 2. Headline & Structural Constraints

1. **Declarative Thesis Headline:**
   - The headline must be a rotunda, sober, and memorable declarative thesis statement.
   - **Maximum Length:** 12 words.
   - **Strict Formatting Ban:** Jacobin América Latina **never uses subtitles**. No colons (`:`), no explanatory dashes, and no rhetorical question headlines.
   - *Model approved by Arboleda:* *"Contra el mito de que el socialismo produce inflación"*.
2. **Word-Count Bounds:**
   - Standard essay range: **1,500 to 2,500 words** (*sweet spot* ~1,800–2,000 words).
3. **Paragraph Dynamic:**
   - 3 to 5 sentences per paragraph (~60 to 90 words).
   - Terminate analytical paragraphs with a dry, decisive punchline (*el remate seco*) fixing the political conclusion.
4. **Contemporary Opening Hook:**
   - The first 2 to 3 paragraphs should anchor the argument in living contemporary dilemmas (e.g., inflation shocks, debt tutelage, living standards).
5. **Strategic Conclusion:**
   - Conclude with a lucid strategic warning or political lesson for transformative movements; strictly avoid academic evasions (*"se requieren más estudios"*, *"el tiempo dirá"*).

---

## 3. Stylistic Register & Vocabulary Blacklist

### 3.1 Anti-Agitprop / Anti-Panfletary Discipline
Jacobin América Latina addresses an educated working-class and intellectual audience. Persuasion rests on the forensic reconstruction of economic facts, balance-of-payments data, and institutional mechanisms.

The following moralizing slogans and visceral clichés are strictly blacklisted:
- `burguesía rapaz`
- `pueblo heroico`
- `lacayos`
- `la lucha continúa`
- `a las calles`
- `odio de clase`
- `planes siniestros`
- `salvaje capitalismo`
- `vilmente`
- `parasitaria oligarquía`

*Rewrite rule:* Replace moral indignation with institutional mechanisms (e.g., capital flight, credit strikes, markups, central bank accommodation).

*Operative Enforcement Boundary:* In automated auditing, this blacklist operates as a heuristic review warning (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`) for authorial prose, not as an undisclosed mechanical failure gate. Explicit quotations, historical citations, and critical dissections of opponent language are detected as quoted context and permitted.

### 3.2 Academic Cliché Blacklist
Eliminate doctoral thesis meta-phrases from opening and closing passages:
- `la historiografía ha establecido`
- `la literatura económica ha debatido`
- `este trabajo analiza`
- `en este artículo sostengo`
- `es necesario seguir investigando`
- `futuras investigaciones deberán`

## 4. Regional Latin American Scope & Thematic Models

1. **Regional Structural Dependency:** Ground the argument in Latin American structural mechanisms: chronic foreign-exchange bottlenecks (*asfixia de divisas*), debt conditionalities, and monetary hierarchy (Alami, Chamorro-Futinico).
2. **Multi-Country Comparisons:** Comparative integration across multiple Latin American countries is an **assignment-specific requirement**, activated when an assignment explicitly requests comparative regional coverage (`--require-latam-comparisons`). It is not an automatic mechanical failure for single-country studies.
3. **Thematic Model (Cost-Shock Inflation):** For articles addressing inflation, markups, and cost-push dynamics, Isabella Weber's *sellers' inflation* framework serves as an approved theoretical reference point.
