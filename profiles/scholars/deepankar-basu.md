## 3. Durable Calibration Principles

### 3.1 Marxian Reproduction Schemas & Classical Foundations
* **Surplus Approach & Growth Theory:** Theoretical arguments must be solidly rooted in the classical-Marxian surplus approach, tracking capital accumulation, profitability dynamics, and reproduction schemas.
* **Leontief Production Foundations:** Enforce output-capital relations formulated via fixed-coefficient or Leontief production functions:
  $$Y_t = \min \{ A_t L_t, \mu_t B_t K_t \}$$
  This foundation explicitly avoids neoclassical smooth marginal substitution and respects the analytical lessons of the Cambridge capital controversy.

### 3.2 Mathematical Consistency & Dynamic Stability
* **Rigorous Analytical Derivations:** Every dynamic growth model, ordinary differential equation (ODE), and transition path must be formally derived with explicit stability conditions and equilibrium properties.
* **Trend-Stabilized Closures:** Long-run trajectories must specify coherent economic closures (e.g., supermultiplier dynamics, capacity adjustments) that maintain mathematical stability without ad hoc parameter tuning.
* **Singularity & Boundary Audits:** Theoretical formulations must be checked for boundary conditions, asymptotic behavior, and singular points (e.g., division by zero or explosive paths at critical parameter thresholds).

### 3.3 Dimensional Analysis & Mathematical Rigor
* **Purging "Log-Dollars" Errors:** Transcendental operations (such as logarithms, exponentials, or powers) can only be mathematically applied to strictly dimensionless ratios. Dimensional consistency audits must ensure variables are properly normalized before log transformation.
* **Unit Consistency:** Mathematical units and currency deflators must balance across all sides of theoretical equations and empirical specifications.

### 3.4 Econometric Time-Series Consistency
* **System-Level Cointegration Restrictions:** Theoretical restrictions must be tested using system-level econometric structures (e.g., Johansen reduced-rank VECM vector restrictions) rather than relying solely on single-equation approximations.
* **Dynamic Consistency:** Empirical specifications must mirror the underlying theoretical differential or difference equations without disconnect between theory and econometric structure.
