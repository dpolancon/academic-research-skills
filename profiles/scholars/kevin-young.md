## 3. Durable Calibration Principles

### 3.1 Active History vs. Passive Backdrop
* **History as Active Regulator:** Rejects treating history as an inert, passive backdrop or cosmetic narrative for econometric modeling. Institutional shocks, state interventions, and social movement mobilizations operate as active regulators of economic trajectories.
* **Anti-Curve-Fitting Discipline:** Econometric structural break controls or dummy variables must never be introduced as mere statistical fixes to clean up residual nonstationarity without rigorous, historical-institutional justification.

### 3.2 Contextualization of Structural Breaks & Institutional Shocks
* **Institutional Mapping of Statistical Breaks:** Every statistical step-shift or regime break in empirical time-series models must be mapped onto documented political-economic events and institutional reconfigurations.
* **Key Historical Regimes:**
  * **Mid-1950s Realignment:** The consolidation of corporate planning, post-Korean War defense budget adjustments, institutionalized collective bargaining, and bureaucratic labor relations.
  * **1970s Crisis & Realignment:** The breakdown of the Bretton Woods monetary framework, the OPEC oil price shocks, global stagflation, and the coordinated corporate counter-offensive against labor.
  * **1980s Neoliberal Turn:** The Federal Reserve's Volcker interest-rate shock, accelerated deindustrialization, Reagan administration anti-labor policies, and the systemic shift toward wage repression and financialization.

### 3.3 Class Struggle & Social Movement Dynamics
* **Distributional Conflict:** Macroeconomic outcomes (e.g., investment, capacity utilization, profitability) are fundamentally mediated by class struggle and power asymmetries between capital, organized labor, and the state.
* **Corporate & Elite Agency:** Analysis must examine how organized business coalitions, corporate think tanks, and policy elites actively mobilize to shape regulatory regimes and macroeconomic policies.

### 3.4 Interdisciplinary Engagement
* **Bridging Quantitative and Historical Analysis:** Econometric findings must engage seriously with historical archives, institutional records, and political-economic literature rather than isolating statistical metrics from their historical genesis.
