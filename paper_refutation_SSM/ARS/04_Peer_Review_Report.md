**Referee Report for *Metroeconomica***

**Manuscript:** "Unbalanced Growth and the Long-Run Limits of the Sraffian Supermultiplier: A Note on Capacity Transformation and Choice of Technique"
**Recommendation:** Accept with Minor Revisions

### 1. General Comments
The revised manuscript represents a major improvement over the previous submission. The authors have systematically addressed all the critical concerns raised in the initial review. 

Specifically, they have:
1. Re-derived the core dynamic system step-by-step from first principles, eliminating any ad-hoc assertions.
2. Replaced the neoclassical Cobb-Douglas production function interpretation of the parameter $\theta$ with a rigorous, classical choice-of-technique micro-foundation based on a mechanization function and cost minimization under a Sraffian distributive schedule.
3. Corrected the mathematical proof, removing the fabricated nullcline and formally resolving the vector field singularity at $\mu=1$ using a desingularized system.
4. Expanded the literature engagement to include crucial debates regarding the stability of the Sraffian Supermultiplier (SSM) and capital-capacity dynamics.

The paper is now a mathematically sound, theoretically consistent, and highly polished note that makes a valuable contribution to the understanding of capacity adjustment and technical change in demand-led growth models. I recommend publication in *Metroeconomica* after a few minor clarifications.

---

### 2. Mathematical Rigor and Derivations

The mathematical foundations are now extremely solid.
* **Derivation of $\hat{\phi}$**: The derivation of the investment share growth rate $\hat{\phi} = \frac{1}{\mu - 1}\hat{\mu} + (1 - \theta)\gamma(\mu - 1)$ is now shown step-by-step from the accounting identity $\phi = g_K v / \mu$, using the logarithmic derivative. By explicitly linking mechanization growth $q$ to the Okishio-Harrod investment accelerator ($q = g_K = \gamma(\mu - 1)$), the authors provide a rigorous macroeconomic foundation for what was previously an ad-hoc equation.
* **Locus of $\hat{\mu}$ and Contradiction**: The steady-state proof is now mathematically correct. The authors show that the stationarity of the investment share ($\hat{\phi}=0$) requires $\mu^* = 1$, while the capacity utilization nullcline ($\hat{\mu}=0$) requires $\mu^* - 1 = \frac{g_z}{\theta\gamma}$. Since autonomous demand growth is positive ($g_z > 0$), these two conditions cannot be satisfied simultaneously, yielding a direct algebraic contradiction ($0 = \frac{g_z}{\theta\gamma} > 0$). This is a elegant and sound demonstration of the non-existence of a steady state.
* **Resolution of the Singularity**: The authors have successfully resolved the singularity at $\mu=1$ by introducing a desingularized system via a time reparameterization ($d\tau/dt = 1/(\mu - 1)$). They prove that the vector field does not vanish at $\mu = 1$, as $d\phi/d\tau = \phi^* g_z \neq 0$. This mathematically refutes the criticism that the non-existence of the steady state was merely an algebraic artifact, showing instead that the trajectories must flow through $\mu=1$ without settling.

### 3. Economic Intuition and Theoretical Consistency

* **Choice of Technique Interpretation of $\theta$**: The introduction of the mechanization function $a^p = f(q)$ and the cost-minimization optimization problem $\max_q [f(q) - q\pi]$ is a brilliant addition. It anchors the capacity transformation parameter $\theta$ firmly within the classical/Marxian theory of technical change and choice of technique along the wage-profit schedule, completely removing any neoclassical aggregate production function connotations.
* **Distributive Bracket**: Section 4 successfully addresses the endogeneity of technical change. By showing that the optimal growth rate of capital-capacity ratio $\xi(\pi)$ depends on the Sraffian profit share $\pi$ (which is determined by class struggle and institutional factors outside the growth system), the authors show that unbalanced growth ($\theta(\pi) \neq 1$) is a persistent, structural outcome rather than a parametric coincidence. This aligns perfectly with Sraffian economics and justifies bracketing distribution for this note.

### 4. Minor Revisions Required

Before final publication, the authors should address the following minor points:
1. **Clarify Labor Growth Assumption**: In Section 2, the authors assume a constant labor force to set $q = g_K$. Please explicitly state this assumption in the text and briefly note how a positive, exogenous rate of labor growth $n > 0$ would modify the optimal mechanization rate (i.e., $q = g_K - n$).
2. **Desingularization Interpretation**: In Section 3, Step 5, add one or two sentences explaining the economic intuition behind the desingularized vector field at $\mu = 1$ (i.e., that when actual utilization is exactly equal to normal capacity, the growth of autonomous demand continuously pulls actual output away from the planned capital expansion rate, preventing the system from resting at $\mu=1$).

**Conclusion:**
This is an excellent revision. The authors have done a superb job of transforming a critically flawed draft into a rigorous and conceptually sound note that meets the high standards of *Metroeconomica*.
