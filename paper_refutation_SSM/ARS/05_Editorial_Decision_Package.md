## Part 1: Editorial Decision Letter

Dear Author(s),

Thank you for submitting your manuscript titled "Unbalanced Growth and the Long-Run Limits of the Sraffian Supermultiplier: A Note on Capacity Transformation and Choice of Technique" to *Metroeconomica*. Your manuscript has been reviewed by three independent reviewers, including the Editor-in-Chief.

### Decision: Major Revision

### Consensus Analysis

#### Points of Agreement (Consensus)
- **[CONSENSUS-4] SC-4: Long-Run Specification Tautology of a Fixed $\theta \neq 1$**: All 4 reviewers agree that fixing the capacity transformation elasticity $\theta \neq 1$ in the long run forces the capital-capacity ratio $v$ to drift indefinitely ($v \to 0$ or $v \to \infty$), which is economically and physically implausible. The author must add a dedicated discussion addressing this limitation and qualifying the results as a medium-run tendency rather than a permanent long-run prediction.
- **[CONSENSUS-4] SC-5: Misspecification of the Investment Accelerator**: All 4 reviewers agree that the Okishio-Harrod accelerator $g_K = \gamma(\mu - 1)$ lacks a trend-growth component (like expected demand growth $g^e$ or autonomous growth $g_z$), forcing the steady-state utilization rate to exceed normal utilization ($\mu^* > 1$) in a growing economy. The author must discuss how a standard SSM investment function (which incorporates expected growth rate adjustments) affects the stability dynamics.
- **[CONSENSUS-4] SC-6: Economic Coupling of Mechanization and Utilization**: All 4 reviewers agree that coupling the long-term technical choice of mechanization growth $q$ directly to the short-run capacity utilization gap $\mu-1$ is economically problematic and creates a logical tension between cost minimization (constant $q^*(\pi)$) and accelerator dynamics (variable $q$).
- **[CONSENSUS-4] SC-7: Labor Force and Employment Dynamics under $\hat{L}=0$**: All 4 reviewers agree that assuming a constant labor force with positive productivity growth implies employment must shrink unless output growth matches productivity growth. The economic rationale and behavior of the model under $n > 0$ must be clarified.
- **[CONSENSUS-3] SC-9: Overly Definitive Tone and Unbalanced Representation**: EIC, R1, and R3 agree that the manuscript's tone is overly definitive regarding the refutation of the SSM and must be tempered to frame the results as a structural challenge. R2 was silent on this point.

#### Points of Disagreement (Splits)
- **Mathematical Correctness of the Singularity and Desingularization (Step 5)**: R1 (Methodology) and R2 (Domain) argue that the singularity at $\mu=1$ is removable and the vector field is smooth when solved explicitly, and that evaluating the system at $\mu=1$ and $\phi^* > 0$ is a logical contradiction. In contrast, EIC and R3 initially praised the desingularization as a rigorous mathematical highlight.
  - **Editor's Resolution**: Side with R1 and R2. The desingularization proof in Step 5 is mathematically incorrect. The author must remove the desingularization and rewrite Step 5 to analyze the levels-based dynamics on the invariant manifold $\mu=1$, which shows that the system cannot rest there.

#### Devil's Advocate Critical Issues
- **DA-CRITICAL 1: Mathematical Error in Desingularization Proof (Step 5)**:
  - *DA's argument*: The desingularized system has a line of equilibria at $\mu=1$ where both $d\mu/d\tau=0$ and $d\phi/d\tau=0$ because $d\phi/d\tau$ contains a factor of $(\mu-1)$ that makes it vanish, invalidating the claim that trajectories flow through without settling.
  - *Corroboration*: Corroborated by R1 W1 and R2 W1.
  - *Editor's assessment*: Valid. The mathematical critique is correct; the desingularization analysis is flawed.
  - *Required response*: The author must rewrite Step 5 of the proof to eliminate the desingularization and instead provide a smooth, levels-based analysis of the dynamics on the invariant manifold $\mu=1$.
- **DA-CRITICAL 2: Logical Contradiction Between Choice of Technique and Investment Accelerator**:
  - *DA's argument*: Capitalists cannot simultaneously choose a constant cost-minimizing mechanization rate $q^*(\pi)$ and a variable mechanization rate $q = g_K = \gamma(\mu - 1)$ driven by the accelerator.
  - *Corroboration*: Corroborated by EIC W3/Q3, R1 Q3, R2 Q2.
  - *Editor's assessment*: Valid. There is an overdetermination/contradiction in assuming capitalists choose $q$ through cost minimization along the wage-profit schedule and also adjust it dynamically via the utilization accelerator.
  - *Required response*: The author must explicitly address this logical tension in the text, clarifying how the model behaves if $q$ is driven by relative factor prices and a secular trend while $g_K$ is driven by utilization.

### Decision Rationale

This discussion note makes a highly rigorous, relevant, and well-structured contribution that directly addresses a central debate in heterodox macroeconomics by integrating classical choice of technique with Sraffian Supermultiplier (SSM) dynamics. By endogenizing the capital-capacity ratio ($v$) via cost-minimizing choice of technique, the note exposes a critical, previously unexamined assumption of the SSM model. All reviewers recommend a Major Revision because the current model is limited by a conceptual "specification tautology" where the capital-capacity ratio is allowed to drift indefinitely ($v \to 0$ or $v \to \infty$) without feedback, which is economically implausible in the long run. Additionally, Reviewer 1 (Methodology) and Reviewer 2 (Domain) identified a critical mathematical error in the desingularization proof of Proposition 1 (Step 5), where a regular, removable singularity is incorrectly treated as divergent. Finally, the use of a trendless Okishio-Harrod accelerator artificially forces the steady state away from normal capacity utilization under balanced growth. The manuscript must be revised to address these mathematical errors, resolve the logical contradiction in the mechanization rate, clarify the economic intuition, and temper the paper's definitive claims.

### Summary of Key Issues
1. **Flawed Desingularization Proof in Step 5 (R1, R2, DA)**: The vector field is smooth and regular at $\mu=1$; the desingularization is incorrect and must be replaced with a levels-based analysis.
2. **Specification Tautology of Long-Run Drift (EIC, R1, R2, R3, DA)**: The model assumes a fixed $\theta \neq 1$ in the long run, forcing $v$ to drift indefinitely. The author must discuss feedback mechanisms (induced technical progress or endogenous distribution) that would stabilize $v$ in the long run.
3. **Incomplete/Trendless Investment Function (R3, DA)**: The investment function lacks a trend-growth component, making normal utilization impossible under steady-state growth. The author must address how standard SSM specifications affect the results.
4. **Logical Contradiction in Mechanization Rate (EIC, DA)**: The rate of mechanization is simultaneously modeled as a constant cost-minimizing rate $q^*$ and a variable rate $q$ driven by capacity utilization. This contradiction must be resolved.
